What changed in v3 and why.

Cut: client-logo trust strip, project-name case studies, named testimonials, email-capture lead magnet. All four required content you don't have and can't honestly fake. Hollow placeholders read worse than not having a section at all — they tell the buyer there should be proof here, but there isn't.

Added: Background section. Tells the truth in three sentences — long senior career at consultancies and SaaS, going freelance now, NDA covers the recent specifics. The "happy to walk through particulars on a call" line is the move: it signals you have real stories worth hearing, just not stories you'll post publicly. Senior consultants handle it the same way; buyers know the pattern.

Reframed: Recent engagements. Anonymized but specific. "Series B SaaS · 2024 · rebuilt the analytics dashboard onto Next.js App Router" tells a buyer everything they need: company stage, recency, project shape, your stack. The company name is the only thing missing, and on a senior site that absence reads as discretion, not weakness — provided the descriptions are concrete. The bracketed examples are templates; replace them with one true sentence each. Resist the urge to inflate.

Testimonials are back, with the LinkedIn count doing the heavy lifting. 25 recommendations is the credibility hammer — the count matters as much as any individual quote, because it proves you didn't cherry-pick. Two strong recs on the page, "+ 23 more on LinkedIn" link below, and the LinkedIn profile linked from the footer. A skeptical buyer can verify in one click without leaving to be convinced. Fill in the two quote brackets with your favorites — pick complementary ones (one speaks to your code, the other to how you work with people, or similar). Specific beats glowing.

Kept: How an engagement works. The process section is doing different work from testimonials — testimonials are social proof ("others survived this"), process is risk reduction ("here's exactly what will happen"). Both matter. The "two weeks of free post-launch support" line in step 04 is a small concrete risk-reducer that converts.

Cut the lead magnet. I've recommended it twice. For your audience — warm Upwork/LinkedIn prospects deciding in minutes whether to book — a PDF download is friction between them and the call. If you want the Claude-workflow PDF anyway, make it a standalone landing page driven by LinkedIn posts, not a section on the homepage.

What this site still needs from you. (1) Two quotes pasted into the testimonials section — pick the strongest two from your 25 LinkedIn recs. (2) Real anonymized descriptions for the three engagement rows — one true sentence each. (3) A photo for the About page when you build it. (4) Optional but recommended: a pricing signal somewhere ("typical engagement $X–$Y") filters out tire-kickers and is more honest than pretending money isn't part of the decision.
